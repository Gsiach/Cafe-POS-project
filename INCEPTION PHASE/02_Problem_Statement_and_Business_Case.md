# Problem Statement and Business Case

## Problem Statement

The café currently operates using **manual transaction recording and informal inventory tracking**. Cashiers calculate totals with calculators and record sales in paper receipt books, while stock levels are estimated visually, and end-of-day reconciliation is performed manually. This leads to **inconsistent financial reporting**, **limited inventory visibility**, increased risk of revenue leakage, longer customer wait times, and operational inefficiencies.

Even minor daily discrepancies — such as errors in 1–2 transactions out of 60–100 per day — can accumulate into **significant annual financial losses**, particularly given small café profit margins (typically 5–15%). Manual inventory tracking also increases the likelihood of **stockouts, overstocking, and spoilage**, affecting both cost efficiency and customer satisfaction.

In Zambia, digital payment and POS adoption are growing, yet **many small retailers and cafés continue to rely on cash and manual systems**. POS transaction volumes in Zambia surged by about **30% in 2024** as consumers increasingly use electronic payments alongside mobile money, which remains the dominant retail payment channel ([Bank of Zambia payment statistics, 2024](https://thebusinesstelegraph.com/zambias-digital-retail-payment-evolution-pos-transactions-surge-30-as-mobile-money-dominates-in-2024/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)). Financial inclusion expanded from roughly 49% in 2018 to over 73% in 2022, yet many small businesses still lack modern POS solutions ([IntelligentCIO, 2024](https://www.intelligentcio.com/africa/2024/03/12/innovative-pos-partnership-changes-zambias-payment-landscape/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)).

At a regional level, Southern and East African countries show similar trends. In Kenya, over **55,000 POS terminals** are in circulation, although small enterprises still face barriers such as cost and infrastructure limitations ([Commerce & Industry Kenya, 2024](https://www.commerceandindustry.co.ke/index.php/2024/10/08/network-launches-in-person-payment-solutions-to-enhance-access-to-payment-in-kenya/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)). Across Africa, SMEs — which make up **over 90% of businesses continent-wide** and roughly **97% in Zambia** — are increasingly turning to digital POS systems to reduce cash handling inefficiencies, improve transaction traceability, and streamline operations ([Bank of Zambia, 2023](https://www.boz.zm/National_Payment_Systems_Vision_Strategy_2023_2027.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)).

Despite these trends, the café continues to rely on manual systems. The absence of an **integrated sales and inventory management system** prevents management from:

- **Generating accurate, auditable financial reports** in real time  
- **Linking sales and inventory movement** to prevent stockouts and overstocking  
- **Monitoring product performance trends** for data-driven decisions  
- **Minimizing waste and spoilage** through better stock control  
- **Reducing customer wait times** and order errors  

In the desired operational state, the café would have **centralized, real-time transaction and inventory data**, enabling faster transactions, reliable reporting, consistent stock control, and improved customer experience. Success can be measured through **reduced reconciliation time, 100% transaction traceability, fewer inventory discrepancies, reduced spoilage, faster customer service**, and **improved scalability** if the café expands.

By addressing these recurring and systemic issues, the café can improve **financial transparency, operational efficiency, customer satisfaction, and long-term sustainability**, aligning with modern POS adoption trends in Zambia, neighbouring countries, and across Africa. ## Business Case

**Executive Summary:**  
Implementing a digital POS system will **solve operational inefficiencies, reduce financial losses, and improve customer satisfaction**, positioning the café competitively in Zambia’s evolving SME retail market. The system is expected to deliver measurable ROI and scalable operational benefits.

### Options Analysis

- **Do Nothing:** Continue with manual processes — risks continued financial leakage, inefficiency, and poor customer experience.  
- **Partial Automation:** Use spreadsheets or simple software — limited efficiency gains, poor integration, low scalability.  
- **Proposed Option:** Full digital POS system integrating sales, inventory, and reporting — **maximizes efficiency, accuracy, and scalability**.

### Recommendation

We **recommend implementing a modern digital POS system** as it addresses financial, operational, and customer challenges while aligning with Zambia’s growing digital SME ecosystem.

### Cost-Benefit Analysis

| Item | Current Cost / Loss | Expected Impact / Savings |
|------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Revenue leakage | $500/month | Reduce 15–20% |
| Staff reconciliation time | 10 hours/week | Reduce by 50% |
| Inventory spoilage | $200/month | Reduce by 30% |

**Financial KPI:** Expected **15–20% reduction in revenue leakage** within the first year.

### ROI Calculation 

**Estimated Investment:** $2,000 (POS system + setup + training)  
**Expected Annual Savings:** $3,000 (Revenue leakage + staff time + spoilage reduction)  

**ROI (%)** = (Savings − Investment) ÷ Investment × 100  
**ROI (%)** = (3000 − 2000) ÷ 2000 × 100 = **50%**

Meaning: Every $1 invested in the POS system returns $1.50 in value annually.

*Note:* Figures shown are **illustrative estimates** for a small café in Zambia. Actual numbers depend on transaction volume, staff, and inventory, but the **percentage improvements are realistic and aligned with trends observed in SMEs across Zambia and Southern Africa**.

### Risk Assessment

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Staff resistance | Medium | Training and ongoing support |
| Power/Internet outage | Medium | Offline POS backup & battery support |
| Upfront cost | Medium | Budget allocation & phased implementation |

### Stakeholder Analysis

| Stakeholder | Role / Interest | Impact / How They Are Affected | Notes / Actions |
|------------|----------------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| **Café Staff (Cashiers, Baristas)** | Daily users of the POS | Will use the system for transactions; need training | Provide hands-on training and ongoing support |
| **Management / Owners** | Decision-makers, monitor financials | Can make data-driven decisions, track sales, reduce revenue leakage | Dashboard access and regular reporting |
| **Customers** | End-users of service | Experience faster, accurate, and reliable service; payment options increase convenience | Communicate POS benefits subtly (e.g., receipts, mobile payments) |
| **Suppliers** | Provide inventory | May interact with system for stock updates or ordering | Could integrate basic stock notifications or reports |
| **IT / Technical Support** | Maintains POS system | Responsible for installation, troubleshooting, and updates | Identify local support or vendor SLA |
| **Regulatory / Tax Authorities** | Compliance | POS generates auditable records, supports VAT or tax reporting | System must comply with Zambian tax laws |
| **Investors / Financiers (if any)** | Funding | Interested in ROI and risk mitigation | Reports or dashboards for financial visibility |
